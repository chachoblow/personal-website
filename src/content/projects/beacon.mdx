Beacon is a small lamp. I made eight of them as Christmas gifts for my family in 2021, during Covid, as a way to stay in touch.

Most of the time a Beacon gives off a dim, warm-white light, and turning the knob on its front changes the brightness. Each Beacon has its own color. Pressing the knob makes all the others flash once in that color. It's a hello to the whole group, and a way to see who else is around: anyone who sees it can press their own to answer. The flash lasts about two seconds and leaves no trace, so it lands only if someone is in the room to see it.

The idea came from the game Journey, where players who meet can't talk and can only call out to each other with a short chime.

The frame is plywood with a translucent white acrylic diffuser. The light unit slides out of its base: an 8×8 grid of RGB LEDs and an ESP32 microcontroller on a 3D-printed chassis, behind an aluminum faceplate. The Beacons reach each other over Wi-Fi through AWS IoT. The [code](https://github.com/chachoblow/beacon) is on GitHub.
